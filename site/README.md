# Landing page

`index.html` asks the visitor where they stand with PHP and hands them the book that starts there, one per persona of `../onboarding personas.md`. Those who only wanted the reference are sent to php.net. It is a draft meant to start a discussion about what a PHP home page could be: its own logo rather than the official one, no official colours, `noindex`.

- The reader is the subject of the page, not the books: the top of the page is a question and two steps of answers, and each book is introduced by what its reader would say. The texts follow the house style of the books (second person, concrete situation first, no sales adjectives).
- One static file, in English. French editions are linked where they exist.
- Book links follow the published layout (`beginner/`, `beginner/fr/`, `beginner/book.pdf`), which `make dist` at the root assembles into `_site/`; `make serve` here serves that folder. GitHub Pages publishes the same folder.
- Two answers give the reader their book in place, at the top of the page: their sentence, the title, a button to start reading, and the matching drawing. `index.html#for-skeptic` opens directly on that answer, which makes each reader's page shareable. Without JavaScript the answers are plain links to the entries below.
- Drawings are described in `illustrations.md` and generated by `make illustrations` into `images/`. Run `make webp` afterwards: the page loads the WebP files first (about 40 KB each instead of 1.2 MB), then the PNG, then art borrowed from the books.
- The search field posts to the `manual-lookup.php` of php.net, which redirects to the page of the function, class or keyword.
