# Landing page

`index.html` asks the visitor where they stand with PHP, under the logo, and shows the five books on one screen, one card per persona of `../onboarding personas.md`. Those who only wanted the reference have the php.net lookup in the header. It is a draft meant to start a discussion about what a PHP home page could be: its own logo rather than the official one, no official colours, `noindex`.

- The reader is the subject of the page, not the books: each card opens with what its reader would say, then the title and the reading time. The texts follow the house style of the books (second person, concrete situation first, no sales adjectives).
- One static file, in English, no JavaScript beyond the image fallback. French editions and PDFs are linked where they exist.
- Book links follow the published layout (`beginner/`, `beginner/fr/`, `beginner/book.pdf`), which `make dist` at the root assembles into `_site/`; `make serve` here serves that folder. GitHub Pages publishes the same folder.
- Five columns above 1180 px, one column of horizontal cards below, and the hero drawing disappears on phones. `index.html#skeptic` scrolls to that card.
- Drawings are described in `illustrations.md` and generated by `make illustrations` into `images/`. Run `make webp` afterwards: the page loads the WebP files first (about 40 KB each instead of 1.2 MB), then the PNG, then art borrowed from the books.
- The search field posts to the `manual-lookup.php` of php.net, which redirects to the page of the function, class or keyword.
- `site-bar.js` repeats the header at the top of every book: the logo back to this page, the manual, and the GitHub repository.
