# Communities Worth Joining

Documentation answers the question you knew to ask. **A community answers the one you didn't.** Every ecosystem in this book has a place where practitioners answer specific problems, usually faster than a search engine finds you a current answer.

- **Laravel**: the Laravel Discord and the [Laracasts](https://laracasts.com) forum are both active, and the official docs at [laravel.com](https://laravel.com) are unusually well maintained release after release.
- **Symfony**: the Symfony Slack and the community forum linked from [symfony.com](https://symfony.com) are where most framework and component questions get resolved.
- **WordPress**: [make.wordpress.org](https://make.wordpress.org) hosts the contributor teams building WordPress core, while the WordPress.org support forums cover plugin and theme questions.
- **TYPO3**: the TYPO3 Slack, linked from [typo3.org](https://typo3.org), gives depth to the enterprise-CMS questions this book only had room to introduce.
- **API Platform**: GitHub Discussions on the project's own repository, linked from [api-platform.com](https://api-platform.com), is where most implementation questions land.
- **Nextcloud**: the community forum and GitHub discussions linked from [nextcloud.com](https://nextcloud.com) cover both self-hosting operations and app development.

## One habit to keep

Beyond any single framework, PHP has an active conference and user group scene, and the major frameworks publish detailed changelogs and upgrade guides rather than surprising you. Follow a framework's release notes, not only its announcements. It is the single most effective habit for staying current without relearning your stack from scratch.

> **Under the hood:** Not applicable here. Communities are made of people, not code, and that is the point. The fastest answer to a stuck problem is often someone who hit the same wall last month, not a deeper read of the source.
